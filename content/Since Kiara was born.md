Kiara was born on May 18, 2026. A little over 4 months from writing this. Palak did amazing and I felt a great bond with the 3 of us that day. I was very excited throughout it all. It was an opportunity for me to step up, and I did. 

In the first few days after bringing Kiara home, I talked to her, showed her everything, especially outside: the bunnies and the chippies. A new role began for me of introducing Kiara to the world and its people.

As for other roles, I find they’re mostly similar to my role of being a husband. I have to give love and care to Palak and Kiara. I feel blessed to see their smiles.

Each week was a learning opportunity. For example, a pediatrician appointment. Each step is centered around Kiara. She must be safely tucked in her car seat and I must avoid too much acceleration. Next, I take the decision to carry her in my hands or the entire car seat. Palak holds the door open and we apply sanitizer after touching the elevator button. We stay a few minutes after the appointment so Kiara can feed. Palak and her are quite flexible and can feed mostly anywhere.

Which brings on a funny new skill: ability to feed or change a diaper anywhere. The dirtiest floor I changed her on was Halo Coffee Roasters, and the cleanest probably Starbucks in Kavi Nagar.

This routine stays the same, but what’s interesting is how Kiara keeps changing. One day you can’t imagine her liking or fitting in the stroller, and 2 weeks later it’s just the right size.

Vocally she was mostly quiet at home, which is hard to imagine now because she starting making many sounds in India. Although it’s not words yet, each grunt or coo carries a lot of expression, allowing us to learn more about who she is. I’m convinced she’s going to stay a chatterbox and enjoy talking in groups like her mom.

I can’t wait for the next 8 months and to see her turn 1 years old. It’s impossible to imagine anything after that. But I’m sure she’ll still be talking and looking at animals, and enjoying her parents company of course.
